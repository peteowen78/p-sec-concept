Can people find Psec online?

We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

1 of 10

Your site passes 1 of the 10 basic checks. Google is free to list the site, but almost everything that helps it do so well is missing. All nine gaps are fixable.

The one that matters most

Browsers tell visitors your site is "Not secure".

Modern websites use a secure connection, shown by a padlock and an address starting "https". Yours does not have one. When we asked for the secure version, the connection failed.

Browsers such as Chrome label sites like this "Not secure" in the address bar. Nothing dangerous is happening, but your clients are finance directors in the NHS and local government, and that label sits badly beside the careful work you describe.

Search engines also prefer secure sites, and a secure connection is normally free to set up.

Eight smaller things to fix

Most of these come from the age of the site, not from anything done wrong. A new site would settle all of them at once.

To fix

The site is not set up for phones

Pages can tell a phone to fit them to its screen. Yours does not, so on a phone the whole page is shrunk to fit and the text is too small to read without zooming.

A new site would read comfortably on a phone, tablet or desktop.

To fix

The page title is "Home"

The title is the blue link people see in Google. Yours says only "Home", with no mention of Psec, the public sector or what you do.

A new site would name Psec and say public sector consultancy and interim management.

To fix

There is no description for Google to show

Under the blue link there are two lines of text. Your site does not supply any, so Google has to pick some words from the page itself.

A new site would say in two lines what you do and who for.

To fix

Shared links show nothing

When someone forwards your link, no title, description or picture goes with it.

A new site would attach all three.

To fix

The page has no headings

Search engines read headings to work out what a page is about. We found none on your home page.

A new site would have a clear heading for each service.

To fix

None of the 19 pictures has a description

Each picture can carry a short written description, used by image searches and read aloud to visitors who cannot see the screen.

A new site would describe every picture.

To fix

Google is not handed your details

Websites can give Google a small fact sheet: what the firm does, where it is and how to reach it. Yours has none.

A new site would include that fact sheet.

To fix

There is no map of the site

Search engines look for a list of a site's pages so they can find them all. Yours does not have one.

A new site would provide it automatically.

What is already working

One thing your current site gets right.

Working

Search engines are welcome

Nothing on the site stops Google from listing it, and the page answers quickly.

Can AI assistants find you?

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude or Google's AI answers instead of searching. These tools read your website too. We checked four things they rely on. Your site passes 2 of 4.

Working

AI assistants are allowed in

Nothing on your site turns them away.

Working

The words can be read without running the page

Your words are there in the page itself, so AI tools can read them.

To fix

Your phone number and address are written out

Neither appears on your home page.

To fix

A summary file for AI tools

There is none. It is a newer idea, a short plain-text summary of what Psec does and who for, and it takes about twenty minutes to write.

Putting your phone number and address on the home page is the simplest fix here.

Nobody can make an AI recommend a business, and anyone who offers to is selling nothing. What a website can do is let these tools reach it, read it and get your facts right.
